\section{Formal verification}\label{app:formalization}

All eight theorems are formalized in Lean~4 (toolchain \texttt{v4.28.0}) on top of Mathlib (pinned to the matching release), in the Lean library of the accompanying repository.
\Cref{tab:lean} lists, for each theorem, the declarations that prove it.
Declaration names are relative to the library's top-level namespace, and \texttt{RP} abbreviates \texttt{RealProbability}.
The library builds from a clean checkout; the listed declarations are checked against explicit target statements in a separate file; and Lean's axiom report shows that they depend only on the standard axioms \texttt{propext}, \texttt{Classical.choice}, and \texttt{Quot.sound}.
The library contains no unproved goals, no added axioms, and no calls to native code evaluation in proofs.

\paragraph{Modelling choices.}
A finite state space is represented as \texttt{Fin n}; since every finite set is in bijection with some \texttt{Fin n}, this loses no generality.
A probability law is a real-valued function that is nonnegative and sums to one.
Kullback--Leibler divergence takes values in the extended reals: it is $+\infty$ exactly when the support condition fails, and otherwise the finite sum with terms of zero mass omitted.
Path laws are built from the transition product, so reversal symmetry under detailed balance is proved rather than assumed.
In the closure theorem the conditional mutual information is defined directly from the joint masses $\mu(x)p_x(y')$, $\mu(x)$, $W_y$, and $\Pr(Y_0=y,Y_\tau=y')$, independently of the variational objective, and the candidate macro kernels range over all Markov kernels.
Graphs are Mathlib's simple graphs, edge fields are real-valued, and closed-walk sums are computed along Mathlib walks.

\paragraph{Results outside the formal development.}
The following supporting results are proved, or cited as standard, in the text but are not part of the Lean library:
the existence of a stationary law and the geometric convergence in \Cref{lem:objecthood_unique_fixed}, and \Cref{cor:eps_ball}, which uses that existence (uniqueness is formalized);
\Cref{cor:forest_reversible};
the converse of \thmNGForceNull{} (\Cref{app:graphexact});
in \thmNGMacroClosureDeficit(iii), the averaging step showing that positive-mass rows which agree pairwise within each fiber equal their fiber average, which yields $\mathrm{CD}_\tau=0$ (the direction from pairwise disagreement to a positive deficit is formalized);
the order-isomorphism part of \Cref{lem:definability-bijection} and the chain-length bound of \Cref{rem:chain_length};
the extension of the arrow bound to memoryless noisy observation in \Cref{rem:arrow_scope};
and the relation between the arrow and entropy production in \Cref{rem:entropy_production}.
Of the examples, the positive closure deficit at time $0$ in \Cref{ex:closure_current_law} is checked formally.
